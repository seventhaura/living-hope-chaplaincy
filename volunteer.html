Volunteer with Living Hope Chaplaincy

Partner with us to give loving encouragement, prayer, and spiritual support to hospital patients and their families when they need it most. Together, we can make a difference.

What volunteers do

A caring presence at the bedside

Our trained volunteers provide encouragement, a listening ear, prayer, and helpful resources to support patients and families on the journey toward healing.

Many patients and their families receive little or no spiritual care when they need it most. Living Hope volunteers visit patients, their families, and healthcare workers, and the average Living Hope volunteer impacts around 2,000 lives a year.

How our volunteers serve

  • We meet every patient where they are and accept them for who they are.
  • We never force religion or our personal beliefs on anyone.
  • We follow all hospital protocols and procedures.
  • We treat every patient, family member, and staff person with the utmost respect.

What it takes

The commitment

Visiting hospital patients and their families is a sacred trust. Here is what volunteering with Living Hope involves.

Once a week

Make hospital visits with Living Hope at least once a week, or more if you like.

2–3 hours a visit

Each weekly visit is about 2–3 hours.

Parking is covered

The hospital you are assigned to pays for your parking.

Immunizations

There is a small initial cost to volunteers for the immunizations the hospital requires.

Screening

Every volunteer is screened three times: by their church, by Living Hope, and by the hospital.

Training

Classroom teaching and role-play, then shadowing when available, with ongoing training and supervision after you start.

How to join

Four steps to your first visit

Living Hope partners with well-established churches, which pre-screen and recommend prospective volunteers. From there, the path looks like this.

  1. 1

    Apply

    Fill out the written volunteer application online.

  2. 2

    Interview

    An in-depth interview with Living Hope President Steve Austin. Only the best applicants are selected.

  3. 3

    Hospital screening

    Your assigned hospital's own volunteer screening: background check, health screening, immunizations, and orientation.

  4. 4

    Training

    Classroom teaching and role-play, followed by shadowing a hospital ministry veteran or staff chaplain when available.

About the training

Our volunteer training was developed in consultation with board-certified chaplains (BCC) from several leading hospitals and is consistent with content taught in Clinical Pastoral Education (CPE) programs. Volunteers continue to receive ongoing training and supervision after they start.
